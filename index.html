Working papers

Corporate Tax Reforms and the Investment Channel of Monetary Policy

Gonzalo Basante, Ezgi Kurt · 2026

Revise & resubmit, Journal of Monetary Economics

Presented at the U.S. Department of the Treasury, Bates College, and the University of Massachusetts Lowell.

Abstract

Firm-level evidence suggests that a one-percentage-point corporate tax cut attenuates the investment response to monetary policy by about 11 percent, for both tightening and easing. A model with borrowing constraints explains the interaction through after-tax internal funds.

The Monetary–Fiscal Policy Understanding Gap: Evidence from an Experiment

Gonzalo Basante, Ezgi Kurt, Jeffrey Livingston · 2026

Revise & resubmit, International Review of Economics and Finance

Presented at the 33rd Symposium of the Society for Nonlinear Dynamics and Econometrics (SNDE), the Bentley University AC–EC–FI Joint Conference, and the Bentley University Department of Economics Seminar.

Abstract

Experimental evidence indicates that the public understands fiscal policy better than monetary policy. Educational materials from the Federal Reserve help narrow this gap, highlighting information frictions in monetary transmission.

Music

Alongside her work as an economist and educator, Ezgi Kurt is also a musician specializing in the musical traditions of Anatolia and Asia Minor. Born in Istanbul into a musical family, she grew up in a house where poetry and music were shared in intimate house gatherings. Active as a performer and cultural worker since her teens, she has accumulated extensive experience in learning and teaching vocal techniques, with a particular interest in vocal improvisation and modal traditions. Her artistic work delves into the musical roots of the world, bridging genres and geographies, guided by a sense of cultural and spiritual depth.

Performances

She has performed at venues throughout the San Francisco Bay Area, including Red Poppy Art House (San Francisco), Starline Social Club (Oakland), the Mondavi Center (Davis), TimnaTal Music (Davis), and Ashkenaz (Berkeley). Her performances have also taken her to venues in the Greater Boston area, including Jordan Hall, Harvard University, Tufts University, and Brown University, as well as Albert Long Hall in Istanbul. Videos of her performances can be found on her YouTube channel.

Musical education

Her musical training began at an early age and has been shaped by studies at various institutions, including the Turkish Radio and Television (TRT, 1996–2001), Mehmet Erenler Baglama Atolyesi (2004), and the Bogazici University Folk Music and Dance Club (BUFK, 2006–2008). During her undergraduate studies at Bogazici University, she took courses in Jazz, Opera and Mythology. In 2009, she joined Cavit Murtezaoglu Ses Atolyesi where she studied advanced vocal techniques, Iranian–Azeri mugams, poetry and improvisation (2009-2015). During this period, she worked as a voice instructor and contributed as a vocalist to the esoteric Sufi music albums Tebrizden Torosa and Ehl-i Hakların Sesi. She also joined the ethnic vocal group Brastam Acapella, performing in numerous concerts, TV programs, and live events.

After moving to the United States in 2015 to pursue her PhD, she continued her musical studies at the Music Department at UC Davis, working in Classical and Hindustani Music Ensembles (2016–2017). In 2025, she was accepted into the Silkroad's Global Musicians Workshop for a week of cross-cultural musical collaboration, where she shared the stage with renowned musicians including Ara Dinkjian, Mike Block, and Jamey Haddad. In 2026, she was featured as a soloist with the Boston Festival Orchestra at Jordan Hall, performing music by Grammy-winning composer Christopher Tin. She is also a member of Grammy-nominated Turkish-American composer Mehmet Ali Sanlıkol’s DÜNYA Ensemble. She serves as Chorus Director of the Boston Meshk Ensemble.
